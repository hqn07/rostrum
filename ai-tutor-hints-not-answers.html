An AI tutor that gives hints, not answers

If a chatbot hands you the solution, you learn less than you think. Ross, the tutor in Rostrum, is built to make you try first.

How Ross works

  • Your try first. Ask for a hint and Ross asks what you have so far.
  • One hint at a time. You get one hint or one step, then another if you ask.
  • The solution is still there. Choose Show the solution when you are stuck. It ends with a similar problem to try yourself.
  • From your own material. Ross answers from your notes, slides and textbook pages, so the help matches your course.
  • Taught for the subject. Each course has a subject, such as physics or chemistry, that shapes the help, the quiz questions and the study guide.

Quizzes that check how sure you are

Quizzes ask Sure, Think so or Guess before the answer. Progress then shows how sure you were against how right you were, including the ones you were sure about and got wrong. Questions you miss come back later in the same quiz, and the next day as flashcards.

What Ross remembers

For each course, Ross keeps a profile in Progress: topics, how many hints you usually need, mistakes that keep coming back and how you like to learn. You can edit it, hide parts of it, clear it or switch it off. It stays on your Mac.

What to know

  • Ross needs an AI engine. We recommend your own ChatGPT or Claude key, which you pay for directly. The free on-device engine is weaker. See the FAQ for costs.
  • With a hosted engine, the text a request needs is sent to that provider. Exactly what is sent, and when.
  • An AI tutor can be wrong. Check answers against your textbook and your professor.
