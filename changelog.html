Changelog

Every release of Rostrum, newest first. Rostrum was called StudyBar before 3.0. Get the latest version.

3.5.2 2026-11-12

  • Update notice: when a new version is ready, Rostrum tells you with a small popover from the menu bar and a bar at the bottom of the window. Nothing installs until you click Update. Rostrum now checks every 6 hours.
  • Free semester of Pro: try Pro free for 4 months from rostrumstudy.app, no card needed. When a trial ends, Rostrum says so once; everything free keeps working, including AI with your own key, and nothing is deleted.
  • Buy Pro: Settings ▸ Plan has a Buy link.

3.5.1 2026-11-12

  • PDFs from Ross: ask for a PDF, sheet or handout and Ross writes the whole thing and offers it as a document card with Open, Save PDF… and Save to Notes. No more "I can't make a PDF", and no more PDFs holding only a one-line summary.
  • Scroll while Ross answers: you can scroll up while an answer is still coming in; "Jump to latest" brings you back. Same in the Assistant and the Notes Ask panel.
  • Export window: the options at the top no longer squeeze together; a divider line (---) prints as a line.
  • Snippets keywords: typing a keyword like ;ext then space now works in email drafts, not just Notes.
  • Blanks: [Name]-style blanks are highlighted and Tab jumps to them, like {name}.
  • Delete: snippets and drafts have a visible Delete button.

3.5.0 2026-11-11

  • Meet Ross: the Tutor has a name. Ask for a hint and Ross asks for your try first, then gives one hint or one step at a time; Show the solution ends with a similar problem to try.
  • What Ross knows: a profile for each course in Progress — topics, how many hints you usually need, mistakes that keep coming back, and how you like to learn. Edit, hide or clear any of it, or switch it off.
  • Taught for the subject: each course gets a subject (picked from its code, changeable in one tap) that shapes Ross's help, the quiz questions and the study guide.
  • How sure are you: quizzes ask Sure / Think so / Guess before the answer; Progress shows how sure versus how right, and the "sure but wrong" ones.
  • Missed questions come back: later in the same quiz, and the next day as flashcards.
  • Better study guides: worked example, then one to finish, then one to do yourself; cover-and-recall questions; a page of your own mistakes; space to write when printed. Choose the length, split into titled parts, and pick what goes in.
  • Better quiz questions: broken or giveaway questions are dropped.
  • Grades: score each item (Exam 1, 42/50), use the syllabus's own letter scale and "drop lowest", see what you need on what's left, and enter a score when you mark work done.
  • Reading: each open book gets its own tab, with Ask about the page you're on, Highlights and Notes beside it.
  • Notes: a sidebar with Pinned, Recent, courses, tags and Archive; a toolbar with labels; and a small format bar over selected text.
  • Snippets: write emails right in Rostrum — drag snippets in, fill the blanks with Tab, and Copy for email keeps bold, lists and links in Gmail, Outlook and iCloud Mail.
  • Today and Insights: Edit to remove, resize, reorder or add widgets.
  • Study: a cleaner look, browser-style tabs, and your sources grouped by Notes, Slides, Textbook and Handouts.
  • Tutor tools: ask Ross for a formula sheet, flashcards, a note, a study plan or a study guide, and it makes it.
  • What's new: the "Rostrum was updated" card shows each change in full.
  • Assistant: works with GPT-5.6 Luna again.

  • Copy and paste: ⌘C, ⌘V, ⌘X, ⌘A and ⌘Z work everywhere again, including Notes and the Tutor.
  • Copy a whole answer: Tutor and Assistant answers have a Copy button.
  • Headings: Heading 1–3 keep their sizes when you stop editing a note.
  • Style menu: each style is shown at its own size.
  • Stop a quiz or exam: end it early and only the questions you did are scored.
  • Done in Notes: saves the note and leaves editing.
  • Notes archive: notes from last term's courses fold into an Archive at the bottom of the list.
  • Notes filter: now sits above the list of notes.
  • Miscellaneous: the Tools group in the sidebar is now called Miscellaneous.
  • What's new: the update window shows what changed (it was empty).

3.0.0 2026-11-04

  • StudyBar is now Rostrum — same app, same data, new name. If StudyBar is still in Applications, quit it and move it to the Trash.
  • Better transcripts from audio files — Transcribe an audio or video file no longer loses words or trails off into garbage with the large Whisper models.
  • A player that follows along — the transcript scrolls with the recording, a tap on any line plays from there, you can show only the starred lines, and the speed buttons light up for the speed you're on.
  • Fix words from the slides — your AI engine finds words the professor says one way and the slides spell another, and they're fixed in the transcript only where they really appear. Each fix is learned into the course and applied to its next lectures.
  • Move a note or file to another course — right-click it in Study's source list ▸ Move to course. Study's sources are now grouped as Notes and Slides & files, each with a count.
  • Clear a study guide — a finished guide has a Clear button (with a confirmation) that takes you back to the start.
  • Faster Study — opening Study with a long study guide no longer freezes for a moment.
  • A new loader — a small grid of dots with a pulse that spreads from the centre replaces the spinning wheel everywhere.
  • A new menu-bar icon — the Rostrum R and cap.
  • Playback speed for everyone — the speed buttons on a lecture player are no longer Pro.
  • Apple Speech stays on your Mac — if Apple Speech can't listen to a language on the device, Rostrum says so instead of sending audio to Apple.
  • Summarize while recording is a setting — Settings ▸ Intelligence. Off, nothing is sent while you record.
  • Recent recordings in Voice Note — your last recordings are listed in Voice Note so you can reopen one without hunting for it.
  • Copy and paste in every module — the Edit menu is put back before every ⌘ key, so ⌘C, ⌘V, ⌘X and ⌘A keep working after you switch modules.
  • µ stays µ when you paste — copying "4.7 µF" from a screenshot no longer turns into "4.7 uF" in your notes.
  • "Getting started" checklist on Today — five steps that tick themselves off, from adding courses to making flashcards. Hide it any time; bring it back in Settings ▸ General.
  • Guide in Settings — short how-tos for new users, including turning on the AI step by step, with a Print button.
  • Open Source tab removed — licenses are under Settings ▸ About ▸ Acknowledgements.

2.13.0 2026-11-02

  • Deadlines agree everywhere — Today, Assignments, Courses, Insights, Board and the badge now count the same overdue and this-week items. The badge shows what's overdue or due today.
  • "Due Nov 2" means by the end of Nov 2 — deadlines from your school calendar that have no time are no longer overdue from midnight, and never show "0 days overdue".
  • Schedule shows the whole week — Monday to Sunday with a weekend column, deadlines at their hour (evening ones too), and red only for what's actually overdue.
  • Online courses aren't Monday 9 AM classes — they no longer appear in Today's classes, on course cards as a 9 AM class, or send Monday class reminders.
  • No doubled Canvas items — the calendar feed and the Canvas connection recognise the same assignment, and a deleted one stays deleted.
  • A deadline at exactly midnight — if Canvas or you set a deadline for 12:00 AM, StudyBar now shows it as due the evening before, which is when it's really due.

2.12.0 2026-11-02

  • See what changed after an update — the first time you open StudyBar after it updates, a short card lists what's new and which version you're on. It never pops up on its own, so it can't interrupt a recording. The full notes stay in Settings ▸ About.
  • Practice questions that teach — each question gives the idea in one line, and hints one at a time if you're stuck. After you answer you see why it works, what each symbol means, the steps, and the common mistake. The timed practice exam keeps none of this, so it stays a real test.
  • Study guides by type of problem — each section gives the idea behind it, how to spot it on an exam, a worked example, one to try yourself, and the traps. The guide ends with a "which tool?" map and the answers.
  • Print practice problems — the physics behind each calculation comes first, with room to work. Hints and solutions are on separate pages at the back.
  • Shared quizzes keep money and minus signs — the web page you send a classmate no longer turns dollar amounts into formulas, and 1200 is no longer accepted for −$1,200.
  • An exponent's sign counts — typing 1.92e13 for an answer of 1.92e-13 was marked right in quizzes. It's now marked wrong, here and on shared quiz pages.

2.11.1 2026-11-02

  • Copy and paste work again — ⌘C, ⌘V, ⌘X and ⌘A did nothing anywhere in StudyBar, because the app's Edit menu was being replaced after launch. They work in every text field, note and AI answer again.
  • Money stays money in notes — in notes with dollar amounts, such as Engineering Economy, two amounts on one line ("$1,000 now and $2,000 later") could turn the words between them into a formula, especially in notes with a table. Amounts now stay plain text and only real formulas are typeset. Notes you already have display correctly without being rewritten, and new AI notes keep money out of math.
  • A missing minus sign is a wrong answer — in a quiz, typing 1200 for an answer of −$1,200 was marked right. The sign now counts.

  • Fixed: a note's title shown twice — a note that begins with its title as a heading showed that title again as the first line of its preview in the Notes list.
  • No "0 sessions" — Time & Focus leaves out its session and focus-time numbers until there's something to count.

2.10.0 2026-10-09

  • The search field filters the list you're on — in Notes, Assignments, Flashcards, Reading, Library, Citations and Snippets, typing in the field at the top right narrows that list as you type. Type part of a course code or name and press ↩ to filter to that course; ⌘↩ searches everything instead. The course chips and the separate search boxes above the lists are gone.
  • Lists work from the keyboard — open a list and use ↑↓ (or j/k) to move, ↩ to open, Space to do the obvious thing (mark an assignment done, review a deck, open a link, copy a citation or snippet), ⌫ to remove with Undo, / to filter and Esc to clear. ⌘1 to ⌘9 open the first nine screens in the sidebar.
  • Quieter rows — the edit and link buttons on a row show when you point at it or select it.
  • ⌘K acts on what's selected — with a note, assignment, deck or other item selected, ⌘K offers its actions first (Make flashcards, Quiz me, Snooze, Review…), then what you used recently, then the screens with their shortcuts. The same actions are in each row's right-click menu.

2.9.1 2026-10-09

Board, Today and the rest open faster too: each note's preview is worked out once instead of on every redraw, dates are formatted without rebuilding a formatter each time, Today picks its top item once instead of once per assignment, and Board only builds the cards you can see.

A number with nothing in it yet — no GPA before a grade, a streak of 0 — is left out instead of shown as "0" or "—"; if a whole row is empty it says so in one line.

  • One toolbar row — each screen's title and buttons now sit up in the window's title bar beside the traffic lights, with the search field always in the same spot on the right — one row of chrome instead of two. Drag the empty part of the bar to move the window. New Tab, New Window and Quit moved to the ⋯ beside Settings at the bottom of the sidebar.
  • One clear button, the rest in ⋯ — every screen has at most one labeled action — *New*, *Review 24*, *Plan my day* — and everything used now and then is in the ⋯ menu beside the title, with words instead of unlabeled icons.

  • A number you click is the list you get — Today's overdue warning opens Assignments ▸ Overdue, and its "this week" number is the one Assignments ▸ This week shows. Attendance and admin you've hidden in Assignments are left out of both, and out of Assignments' own numbers; "housekeeping hidden" counts only what the list you're on leaves out. Today's list for the day no longer repeats the item at the top.
  • The title bar behaves like one — double-clicking it does what System Settings says (Fill, Zoom, Minimize or nothing), a window in the background moves on the first drag, and beside a narrow page such as an assignment's form the bar and its line run the full width of the window.
  • Smaller fixes — a course card names work due exactly a week out by its date, and counts it the same whether the course has a grade or not; a card with no course code or grade no longer starts with an empty strip. Reading's header leaves out a 0-day streak and other zeros. Insights' average no longer covers today's study time. *Schedule* and *Library* keep their titles in the narrowest window. Settings ▸ Modules shows Settings as pinned at the bottom instead of offering a star and arrows that did nothing. A note's lines are the same length while writing as while reading.

2.6.0 2026-10-07

  • All of Study in sight — at the window's usual size, Study's tabs had folded into a menu reading "Tutor", hiding Quiz, Exam and Progress. They're now all on screen.
  • Formulas show while you edit — a formula written with \dfrac (as the AI usually writes them), \tfrac, \operatorname or \boxed was a blank line in the editor, though it showed in the reading view. It's drawn now.
  • VoiceOver names every button — icon-only buttons and menus (⋯, pin, back, show and hide, delete) now say what they do, and a toggle says which way it will go.
  • Fixed: a stray back button — opening a deck, a card or an editor put a second back arrow over the window's close and minimize buttons.
  • Fixed: a ⭐ nobody gave — study notes could star a question when nothing was starred in the lecture; a ⭐ in the notes now always means you starred it.
  • Homework and deadlines from a lecture, into Assignments — study notes from a lecture now end with what the lecturer announced (problem sets, readings, quizzes, exams) and a list of every formula with what its symbols mean. The note offers *Deadlines*: each announced item as an assignment, dated from what was said ("due next Wednesday at midnight", counted from the lecture's day), to check and add.
  • Fix a misheard word everywhere — in Voice Note, *Fix a word…* corrects a term the transcription got wrong ("ferrets" → "farads") throughout the transcript before you save. In Notes, *Fix a Word in Every Note of the Course…* (in the note's menu, or right-click the word) does it across a whole course, with Undo. Either can teach the course to expect the right word in future recordings.
  • Recording expects the course's words — speech recognition is now also told the slide titles of the lecture's deck, your term :: definition lines, your flashcard terms and the syllabus's key words — and a recording started from the menu bar or ⌃⌥R gets them too.
  • More for a selected passage — right-click a selection in a note for *Ask About…*, *Summarize Selection* and *Highlight*, beside *Explain* and *Make Flashcards*.
  • Search every card, and pause one — the Flashcards screen searches every deck's cards at once. *Pause Card* keeps a card in its deck but out of review until you resume it.
  • Deadlines in Calendar — Assignments can add your open deadlines to Calendar, or save them as a file for Google Calendar or Outlook. End-of-day deadlines show as all-day events, each with a reminder the day before.
  • A slide's cards — beside a lecture's note, each slide shows the flashcards made from it, and *Make cards* writes more from what the notes say about that slide.
  • Fixed: quiz sources — a question's source read "the [notes, part 1] source"; it now reads as the source.
  • Progress says what to do next — Study's Progress tab opens with one next step and the button that does it: sit a practice exam when a test is this week, review the flashcards that are due, quiz yourself on your weakest topic or on a syllabus objective your notes cover but no quiz has asked about, make flashcards from notes that have none, or take a first quiz.
  • Quizzes as hard as you want — Quiz and Practice exam have Easier, Standard and Harder. Easier sticks to definitions and one-step questions for a first pass; Harder asks multi-step problems and builds the wrong answers from the mistakes students really make. *Quiz me* on a note uses the last one you picked.
  • Flashcards of the kind you want — *Make flashcards* now asks what kind: Mixed, Terms (a term on the front, what it means on the back) or Fill in the blank — and how hard: Easier, Standard or Harder.
  • Stop anything that's taking too long — study notes from a lecture, quizzes, practice exams, study guides, study packs and audio reviews can be cancelled, from their own screen or from the bar under the window. *Make flashcards* has Stop too, and keeps the cards it has written so far.
  • No dead end in Study — a course with nothing in it yet says so, and offers to add slides or files, record a lecture, or switch to a course that has material, instead of a Start button that could only fail.
  • Nothing lost to one click — deleting a link, citation, snippet or Read-later item can be undone, and it waits in the Trash. Removing or clearing the Shelf shows Undo. *Remove PDF* on a book asks first, and the PDF goes to the Trash rather than disappearing.
  • Fixed: Study ran off both edges of a narrow window — once a course had quiz scores, Study in a small window pushed its sources under the sidebar and the tabs off the right. In a narrow window the course and its sources now sit behind a button above the tabs.
  • Fixed: search found everything — searching "ohm" matched nearly every note, because those letters appear in order somewhere in most paragraphs. Search now only counts letters that sit close together.
  • Record from anywhere — start, pause, resume and stop a lecture from the menu bar, or with a shortcut (⌃⌥R by default, in Settings ▸ Shortcuts with the others). If there's an unsaved recording, Voice Note opens and asks first.
  • Note a moment without stopping — while recording, type a note ("on the exam", "ask about step 3") in Voice Note or from the recording bar on any screen. It goes into the transcript right where the lecture is, marked 📝, plays back from that moment, and study notes keep it word for word.
  • Transcribe a file, easy to find — *Transcribe a file…* is on the Voice Note screen, whichever engine you use.
  • Lectures titled by topic — a lecture saved straight from its transcript is named for what it's about ("Week 5 — Capacitors and Stored Energy"), not by its first sentence.
  • Search inside your books and course files — the search box and ⌘K find words inside your textbooks and course files, showing the page and the words around them; a result opens the book at that page. Search results now open the note, deck or book itself rather than just its section, and ⌘K finds flashcards and books too.
  • Flashcards that know where they came from — a card made from a note remembers the note and the moment in the lecture; from a book, its page. The card list shows it, and in review, after the answer, one click opens the note and plays the lecturer saying it. A note's footer counts the cards made from it and opens their deck.
  • Flashcards from your notes, where you'd look — every note has *Flashcards* (and *Quiz me*) beside *Ask*; right-click a selection for *Make Flashcards from Selection*; and Flashcards opens with *Make cards from notes*. Pick the notes (a lecture, a week, the whole course), say how many and what to focus on, check and edit the cards, and add them to the course's deck — with Undo.
  • Study notes your way — *Make study notes* now asks how: Brief, Standard or Full; as Notes, an Outline, Cornell notes or Q&A; with nothing, a little or a lot filled in by the AI; and anything to stress, like "formulas" or "what's on the exam". It remembers your choices, and *Rewrite* writes them again from the original transcript. A transcript you've already saved can be turned into study notes from its note.
  • After a lecture, what's next — a lecture saved from Voice Note offers *Study notes*, *Flashcards* and *Quiz me* right on the note. *Quiz me* opens Study on that course with a quiz written from the note.
  • Study opens on your course — the course you're working in, or the class in session, instead of the first course in the list. Flashcards puts that course's decks first.
  • Fixed: Study cut off in a small window — at the window's default size the Study tabs pushed the quiz off the right edge; a narrow window now shows the tabs as a menu.
  • Pause a recording, then carry on — Voice Note now has Record, Pause and Stop. Pause turns the mic off for a break; Resume continues the same recording and the same transcript, and the clock stands still while you're paused. The bar under every screen and the menu-bar clock show it too.
  • Nothing lost by a stray tap — recording again, transcribing a file over a transcript you haven't saved, or Discard now ask first: *Save as Note, Then Record*, or discard — and a discarded recording goes to the Trash with its transcript, never straight to nowhere.
  • A crash or a dead battery no longer loses the lecture — the audio is saved in a form that survives being cut off, and Voice Note offers the interrupted recording back — transcript, audio, starred moments and all.
  • Fixed: a quiet stretch ended the recording — a few minutes of silence in a lecture stopped it with "Apple Speech isn't producing any text". It now keeps listening.
  • Fixed: an error hid your transcript — when a recording stopped with a problem, the error screen hid the transcript and *Try again* wiped it. It now stays on screen to save.
  • Read your textbook in StudyBar — a book with its PDF attached gets *Read*: the PDF opens in Reading at your page and keeps your place, so the chat beside it (⌘J) always knows the page you're on. Select text and *Highlight* (⇧⌘H) saves it to the book's highlights with its page — ready to become flashcards.
  • A glossary of each course — Study's new Glossary tab lists every term in the course with its definition, gathered from your notes (term :: definition lines, and the bold term-and-definition lines study notes are written in) and your flashcards, A to Z and searchable. *Make flashcards* adds the terms that don't have a card yet.
  • What your syllabus covers, and what you haven't — Study ▸ Progress now opens with the course's syllabus coverage: each learning objective with the notes, flashcards and quiz answers you have on it, and the gaps marked.   • The tutor sees the page — with ChatGPT or Claude, the tutor now looks at the actual slides and textbook pages its answer comes from, so graphs, diagrams and typeset equations count — not only the words on them. Each answer lists the pages it was shown.
  • Answers your way — Settings ▸ Intelligence ▸ Answers: short or detailed, pitched at your level, and in the language you choose. It applies to the tutor, Ask this note and the assistant; rewriting and proofreading keep your note's own language.
  • Essay help — a note's AI menu has a new Essay section: outline an essay from your topic or thesis, get feedback on the thesis (with two stronger versions), see the strongest counter-arguments, or draft a paragraph from your notes.
