Record and transcribe lectures on your Mac

Press record in class, and Rostrum writes the lecture down as it happens. Afterwards you have the audio, a full transcript and notes you can edit.

How it works

  1. Open Rostrum and start a Voice Note before the lecture begins.
  2. Leave your Mac on the desk. The transcript fills in while you record. Speech recognition runs on your Mac and your audio is not sent anywhere.
  3. When you stop, the recording is saved with the note. Tap any line of the transcript to play the audio from that point. Star the lines that matter.
  4. If you have set up an AI engine, Rostrum turns the transcript into clear study notes you can read and edit.

What it can record

  • The microphone, for a lecture in a room.
  • The sound your Mac is playing, for a Zoom or Teams class or a video. The first time, macOS asks for permission.
  • An audio or video file you already have.

Accuracy

Rostrum can use Apple Speech or Whisper. Both run on your Mac. Whisper models download once, and the largest ones are more accurate and slower. The most accurate models are part of Rostrum Pro. Accuracy still depends on the room, the microphone and the speaker, so sit near the front if you can. Rostrum also tells the recognizer about your course's own terms, such as slide titles and your flashcard terms, which helps with technical words.

Before you record

Check that you are allowed to record. Rules differ by school, and some professors want you to ask first. See the FAQ.
